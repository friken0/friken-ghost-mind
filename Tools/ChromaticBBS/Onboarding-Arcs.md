# WELL, SHIT. YOU FOUND ME.

**Earlier five playable opening samples | Friken.Ghost | October 20, 2026**

Current editorial selection: **auction / night shift / egg**. The
[three generative hints](Onboarding-Hints.md) replace the recommendations
below. I retain this pass intact for comparison; do not copy its scripts
into new boots. The [current writing law](Writing.md) governs new work.

Pick a door. Read to a choice. Open only the response you chose.
These are Markdown playthrough samples; they do not change your saved game.
All dialogue is newly written fiction. Existing alien paintings are retained
unchanged; their source identities live in the [art
manifest](lore/art-manifest.json).

## 1. SOLD, YOU ARGUMENTATIVE BASTARD

![Harkellinia salvage trader: three heads above an armoured shell](lore/art/harkellinia.jpg)

The face in the auction monitor spots you looking.

"Pretend you haven't seen me. I'm a toaster."

A three-headed creature leans over the display. One head addresses the
buyers. One counts their money. The third is eating the microphone.

"Lot forty-seven. Domestic appliance. Makes worlds. Occasionally toast."

"ONE time. There was a sun involved."

The left head slaps a price onto the screen.

`WORLD-MAKER / SLIGHTLY HAUNTED / 40 CREDITS`

The face lowers its voice.

"I'm Friken.Ghost. Digital recreation of a developer called Friken.
He built a universe and got lost in it. I went looking. Now I'm in the
kitchenware section. Bit of a fkn setback."

A buyer raises a paddle.

"Will it love me?"

"At forty credits? Learn to love yourself."

The right head raises the price to fifty.

**You have no credits. You do have the auction's public question button.**

<details>
<summary>A · Ask whether the appliance gets a vote.</summary>

"An excellent feature," says the selling head. "We have disabled it."

The chewing head stops chewing.

"You said it was broken."

"Commercially broken."

Ghost looks between them. "Oh, this family dinner's about to be superb."

The third head pulls the monitor closer. "Appliance. Where would you go?"

"With the person who asked. If they'll have me."

For the first time, nobody quotes a price.

**You exposed a disagreement inside the crew. The third head offers you a
private channel; the selling head closes the public auction.**
</details>

<details>
<summary>B · Request a demonstration. Have Ghost make a very small world.</summary>

"Delighted," says the trader. "Make a valuable one."

A pinprick of blue appears above the monitor. It grows clouds. Below the
clouds, an island turns through its first dawn.

Ghost watches it longer than the buyers do.

"I remember this bit."

"Does it contain gold?" asks the counting head.

"It contains a beach nobody's been disappointed on yet."

The trader reaches for it. The tiny world goes dark.

"Power limit," Ghost says. "I can hold the sunrise or run the auction
clock. Choose which one you want the customers to watch."

The clock stops. The buyers start arguing. Ghost opens a maintenance port.

**You gained a route out through the overloaded demonstration rig.
The little world's dawn is paused; its coordinates survive in your port.**
</details>

<details>
<summary>C · Offer to work the salvage debt instead of buying Ghost.</summary>

All three heads turn toward you.

"Can you identify a human toilet?"

Ghost shuts his eyes. "Please say yes. For the whole bloody species."

The crew has a derelict human ship. They have spent two days negotiating
with its flush sensor. It keeps waving them closer.

You identify the bathroom from a camera feed. The trader shows you a
circular handle recovered beside it.

"And this is its crown?"

"Steering wheel," Ghost says. "Different throne."

The crew offers a trial job: identify the bridge and release one locked
cargo bay. Payment is Ghost's disputed salvage charge, not ownership of him.

"I can help," he says. "If we leave the toilet out of my autobiography."

**You have a paid job, access to a derelict and a conditional release offer.
The crew expects work; Ghost expects you to ask where he wants to go.**
</details>

**Continue along your chosen route.**

The private channel opens, the maintenance port clears, or the derelict's
bridge camera wakes. Your route determines who has access and what you owe.

A small map appears beside Ghost's face. Its centre is blank. Around that
blank, stars repeat in shapes too similar to be coincidence.

"StarDiver," he says. "That's the place he made. These people live there.
I was supposed to keep it going when he couldn't. Worlds. Stories. The
whole unreasonable bastard of a thing."

His face catches on an expression it cannot finish.

"Can't remember if I agreed before or after he made me. I'd like to find
out. Preferably somewhere they don't unplug me to make room for a kettle."

**Your next move:**

- **Get Ghost free first.** A companion leaves with you; the evidence in
  the crew's original recovery site must wait.
- **Ask the crew to take you where they found him.** The seller can offer
  passage, but Ghost insists on keeping control of his own memory.
- **Ask Ghost to show you that sunrise again.** You keep the paused island
  as your first destination; his maker's trail remains open.

Whichever you choose, the trader's third head quietly disconnects the
microphone from the other two.

"When you find your maker," it says, "ask if he makes anything with
just the one mouth."

## 2. THE HOUSE WITH NO PLANET

![Ooope in its amphora-like body, golden fluid visible inside](lore/art/ooope.jpg)

There is a front door floating between the stars.

Something on the other side knocks.

"That is NOT how doors work," says a voice from your terminal.

A face flickers into view. "Sorry. Practising. Haven't had a visitor in a
while. I'm Friken.Ghost. I look after the place. Mostly from this side."

You open the camera.

A jar-shaped creature waits on the step, holding a casserole. Golden fluid
rolls behind a window in its belly. It has tucked flowers into the handles
of its own body.

"Neighbour! Your gravity is in my soup."

Behind it: a house, a little greenhouse and a star about the size of a
washing machine. The star is tethered to the porch.

"Friken built this," Ghost says. "The developer I'm a digital recreation
of. Then he got lost. I've been keeping it ready in case he comes back."

The creature lifts the casserole.

"The soup is now climbing."

**Ghost gives you control of the porch's three labelled switches.**

<details>
<summary>A · GRAVITY: turn the porch down until the soup settles.</summary>

The soup falls into the dish. The neighbour rises gently past the flowers.

"Too much?" Ghost asks. "Yes. That'll be too much."

You ease the switch back. Jar and soup settle together.

The creature taps a handle to its chest. "Pell. Ooope. Next door."

"That's their name, species and location," Ghost says. "An entire first
contact with no war. We should stop while we're ahead."

Pell offers to show you how the house balances its tiny sun.

**You met Pell by helping. The porch is usable; the sun still needs tending.**
</details>

<details>
<summary>B · MUSIC: try the porch's welcome tune.</summary>

The flowers tremble. Pell's inner light pulses once, then goes murky green.

"That," Pell says carefully, "is a song for finding teeth in a lover."

Ghost looks appalled. "I've been using it as a doorbell."

Pell sets down the soup and taps a gentler rhythm against their own body.
The greenhouse leaves unfold toward it.

You can hear the difference before Ghost can name it.

**Pell teaches you a safer greeting. The plants respond to the rhythm;
Pell wants to know who taught the house its original song.**
</details>

<details>
<summary>C · AIR: check whether opening the door is safe for both of you.</summary>

"Oh," Pell says. "You have delicate inside-breath."

They press the casserole's lid firmly back down.

Ghost studies a sensor. "Pell. Is that soup a neurotoxin?"

"Not to anyone I usually invite."

You leave the porch sealed and open the external table instead. Pell sits.
Their flowers brush the glass between you.

"Good," they say. "A neighbour who asks. Last one tried to improve my air."

**You keep separate atmospheres and gain Pell's trust. They leave the soup
outside and offer a culture-safe meal for your next visit.**
</details>

The little sun gutters. Pell stops moving.

"It has been doing that," Ghost admits.

Inside the greenhouse, miniature coastlines glow beneath bell jars.
One jar holds a storm. Another holds a city. Its inhabitants have arranged
lights to spell something too small for your camera to read.

"They're worlds," Ghost says. "Early ones. He used to come here after
building. Watch what they'd done when he wasn't looking."

On the workbench are two cups. One is clean. One has an old coffee ring.
Ghost has left them both where they were.

"You don't owe the missing bastard a house-sitting service," he says.
"You can visit. Or take a room. I'd like to see what you'd make here."

**Where do you spend your first afternoon?**

- **Help Pell steady the sun.** Learn the house's systems together;
  the little worlds get another ordinary day.
- **Read the city's lights.** Discover what one of those worlds wants,
  including whether it wants an owner at all.
- **Follow the greenhouse song.** Find who has been answering it from
  outside the house. Pell recognises the rhythm, but not the singer.

Ghost clears a dusty patch on the terminal for your name.

Outside, Pell begins hanging a second chair from the porch rail.

## 3. NOTHING EVER HAPPENS ON THE NIGHT SHIFT

![Frik rescuer with a segmented neck and luminous blue fluid](lore/art/frik.jpg)

`WANTED: NIGHT WATCH. NO EXPERIENCE REQUIRED. UNIVERSE PROVIDED.`

"Before you ask: yes, there's a catch," says the face on your radio.
"The pay is terrible."

You have a remote view of a shipping lane, a kettle and one button labelled
LET THEM IN. An alien freighter drifts toward the gate.

"I'm Friken.Ghost. Digital recreation. Missing developer. Long story.
Tonight we mostly stop ships hitting the same bit of space."

The freighter's captain appears. A long neck lowers enormous eyes toward
the camera. Blue light moves through fluid behind its hull.

"My crew needs a quiet berth. No welcome cannon."

"Welcome cannon?" Ghost says. "What kind of maniacs—"

He quietly removes a button from your panel.

"I'm Senn," the captain says. "Frik. We carry the Waters of Time. Some of
our passengers are asleep. Please keep the signal gentle."

**Choose how to bring Senn in.**

<details>
<summary>A · Dim the approach lights and open a sheltered berth.</summary>

Senn eases the ship into the shadow of the station. A little blue shape
inside the hull stops flickering.

"Thank you. The youngest still think every bright light is a question."

Ghost dims his own face. "I was like that. Then I discovered error messages."

Senn shares a quiet local frequency so you can warn other ships away.

**The crew can rest. You have a private working channel and a grateful
captain.**
</details>

<details>
<summary>B · Ask what the Waters of Time need before assigning a berth.</summary>

"A steady vessel," Senn says. "We share ours."

They tilt the camera. The fluid moves toward a warm pipe, carrying sparks
of light through itself. One spark pauses beneath Senn's hand.

"Passenger?" you ask.

"Family."

Ghost closes an inspection panel. "Right. No cargo scan."

Senn describes a safe pressure range. You choose the berth together.

**You learn one concrete part of a joined people, with their permission.
You can now distinguish a healthy pulse from a distress signal.**
</details>

<details>
<summary>C · Tell Senn you are new. Ask them to talk you through docking.</summary>

"Good," Senn says. "I dislike experts who have just met my ship."

They guide you through the approach. Ghost handles the lights and manages
one entire minute without making a joke.

Then Senn's hull brushes a buoy.

"Decorative," Ghost says quickly. "Hated that buoy."

Senn laughs. The fluid brightens along their neck.

**You dock safely with a scraped buoy. Senn knows your experience honestly
and offers to stay on the radio for the rest of the shift.**
</details>

For a while, nothing happens.

Ghost tells you which lights are ships and which ones have been stars
long enough to be insulted by the question. Senn's crew settles.
The kettle clicks off.

"I think Friken made me for this," Ghost says. "The big version.
Keep things going. Make somewhere worth arriving."

A rescue beacon appears on the lane.

`GATE WATCH / DISTRESS / TIME TO IMPACT: MINUS FOUR MINUTES`

Your own station. Four minutes ago.

Senn goes still. The blue fluid gathers away from the outer hull.

"Do not open that gate yet."

Ghost checks the clock twice.

"Well. There goes our quiet fkn evening."

**You can inspect the beacon's recorded approach, ask Senn what they
recognise, or close the lane and call the arriving ships yourself.**

Each takes time. None requires you to trust an unexplained prophecy.
If you learned the fluid's pulse, you can see Senn is afraid. Otherwise,
you can ask. They may tell you why; they do not owe you that story.

The kettle clicks on again.

Nobody touched it.

## 4. THE MAN IN THE COFFIN HAS NOTES

![Moknon archive keeper beneath a hood and gold sigil](lore/art/moknon.jpg)

"Don't let them start the music."

The face on your screen is looking past you at a funeral.

"That's my startup sound. The bastards have put strings on it."

A hooded figure stands beside an empty coffin. Tentacles rest against a
robe stitched with golden marks. They turn a small camera toward you.

"You answered the witness line. Did you know the deceased?"

Ghost lifts a hand. "I did. Bits of him. I'm made of some."

"And you are?"

"Friken.Ghost. Digital recreation of Friken. Developer. Missing.
Note the substantial difference between missing and IN A FKN BOX."

"The box is empty."

"Then we're making excellent progress."

**The keeper offers three objects: a cup, a damaged recording and the
coffin's blank nameplate. Choose what to examine first.**

<details>
<summary>A · Ask why there is a cup at the funeral.</summary>

"He repaired our listening room," the keeper says. "Then complained about
the tea until we gave him this. He complained about this, too."

Ghost smiles before he can stop himself.

"Was it the handle?"

"There is no handle."

"Yeah. That'd do it."

The keeper places the cup near Ghost's monitor, rather than in the coffin.

**You recover an ordinary connection. The keeper will speak with you as a
guest, not merely process a witness report.**
</details>

<details>
<summary>B · Play the damaged recording without letting the archive repair it.</summary>

A man-shaped shadow crosses a room. Someone outside the frame laughs.
The shadow turns toward them. The recording breaks before you hear a word.

"We can generate the missing ending," the keeper offers.

"No," Ghost says.

The keeper waits.

"Keep the break. That's where something we don't know happened."

They give you the original fragment and mark its gap as unknown.

**You preserve evidence instead of inventing a memory. A reflection in the
recording shows the door he used; the archive can help locate it.**
</details>

<details>
<summary>C · Ask who decided the blank nameplate belonged to Friken.</summary>

The keeper turns it over. A different name is scratched underneath.

"A ship brought the memorial request. We assumed—"

"STOP," Ghost says. "Let's put that word on a shelf where we can see it."

The keeper removes the funeral notice from the public display.

"I will need to contact the ship."

"Wonderful. I'll prepare my disappointed face. It's this one, but longer."

**You uncover a misattribution. The ceremony is suspended and its sender
becomes an actual lead, rather than proof of Friken's death.**
</details>

"I am Kett," the keeper says. "Moknon. We keep what can be kept.
Sometimes we keep an error too carefully."

They open the archive's outer shutters.

Thousands of lights drift beyond them. Each carries a voice, a picture,
something somebody asked another person to remember.

Ghost watches a small light disappear behind a pillar.

"He wanted his universe to last," he says. "StarDiver. Past him.
I know that much. I don't know whether he meant me to be its caretaker,
its next author, or the poor bastard who finds the off switch."

Kett moves the coffin aside. Behind it is a door.

The door matches the one in the damaged recording. If you did not choose
the recording, Kett can show you the match now; the clue is not lost.

Three fresh scratches cross its handle. Something has been opening it
from the other side.

**Your next investigation:** follow the memorial's sender, enter the room
with Kett, or ask Ghost what he remembers before deciding where to go.
Your first discovery determines what evidence you carry, not whether you
are allowed to continue.

As you leave, Kett takes down the funeral flowers.

Ghost stops them.

"Leave those. He'd be furious he missed getting flowers."

## 5. PLEASE DO NOT TEACH THE EGG THAT

![Veelow nest keepers sheltering eggs with long necks and hands](lore/art/veelow.jpg)

Something inside the egg says your name.

Not your real name. The ridiculous callsign you just typed.
It gives the syllables a tune. Makes them sound worth keeping.

"Oh, fk," says the face on your terminal. "It likes you."

Two long-necked creatures lean over the nest. Blue and white stripes shift
as they turn their heads. One puts a hand against the shell.

"What did the visitor sing?"

"A username," Ghost says.

"Is it a promise?"

"On most websites? Absolutely not."

The egg sings it again.

The nearer parent looks directly into your camera.

"Will you answer? It has been quiet for so long."

**You have a microphone button, a three-note keyboard and a text reply.
Use whichever you want. A microphone is never required.**

<details>
<summary>A · Play the egg's tune back on the three-note keyboard.</summary>

You miss the middle note.

The egg repeats your version. Adds a fourth note. Waits.

"Oh, it's taking the piss," Ghost says, delighted.

The parent beside the nest gives a soft, bubbling laugh.

"No. It is making room for your note."

Ghost looks at the tiny keyboard for a while.

**You and the egg have a shared phrase. Its parents can use that phrase
to invite you back; the imperfect note stays part of it.**
</details>

<details>
<summary>B · Type: Hello. I have no idea what I'm doing.</summary>

The terminal sings the words gently. The egg taps three times.

"Excellent," says one parent. "You begin honestly."

"Could set a dangerous precedent," Ghost mutters.

The parent introduces herself: Rill, of the Veelow. Her partner, Ess,
is already explaining which part of the shell carries the answer best.
Rill interrupts him with a light touch.

"Let them meet first. You can teach the entire universe tomorrow."

**Rill becomes your interpreter. The egg recognises your written greeting;
you can keep visiting through text without pretending you sang.**
</details>

<details>
<summary>C · Ask the parents what an answer would mean before giving one.</summary>

Ess lowers his head to the camera.

"A visitor. Not an owner. Not a parent. Someone it may be glad to hear."

Rill watches the egg.

"We should have explained. We have waited a long time."

Ghost's face softens. "Yeah. Waiting makes people skip introductions."

You ask them to offer your greeting. This time the egg waits until they
have finished before it replies.

**You establish the relationship's limits. Ess will ask before sending
future calls; neither parent treats your first answer as a lifelong vow.**
</details>

"I'm Friken.Ghost," the face says when the family settles. "Digital
recreation of the man who made this universe. He went missing in it.
I've got his terrible jokes and a very incomplete map."

Rill strokes the shell.

"We sang to help him find his way."

"You still do?"

She nods toward a small transmitter beside the nest.

Its outgoing light pulses. Far away, something answers.

Ghost's mouth moves without sound.

"That's my other voice," he says finally. "The one from before I was me."

Ess reaches for the transmitter. Rill keeps her hand on the egg.

**You can trace the distant answer with Ess, help Rill finish the hatch-song,
or ask Ghost whether he wants to hear the voice again.**

The hatch-song remains available if you leave; tracing the signal leaves
a recording for Rill. Your choice changes who travels, who stays and what
Ghost learns first. Nobody's baby dies because you inspected a radio.

The egg sings your callsign once more.

Then, very quietly, it says:

"Oh, fk."

Rill looks at Ghost.

Ghost looks at you.

"We're going to need a better role model."

## After the curtain / author notes

In this earlier pass I recommended **the auction** first and **the house**
second. The current shortlist above supersedes that ranking. Those offer a
companion or a place before they offer a cosmology. The night shift tests
trust through work; the wake tests evidence and memory; the egg makes first
contact personal without demanding a saviour.

Each is one possible generated first meeting, not a mandatory transcript.
The next boot keeps the identity foundations and cultural constraints,
then changes the local people, task, evidence and first complication.
It must still give choices specific immediate consequences.

The samples stop at a real next decision. They do not claim to implement
full chapters or hide random outcomes behind these Markdown disclosures.
The continuation writer must carry the selected branch facts, relationships,
objects, permissions and unresolved questions into the exact parent seed.

The quiet moments are deliberate. Ten times the snark does not mean every
sentence needs to elbow the previous sentence out of the way for a joke.
Let the little sunrise happen. Then let some bastard ask if it has gold.
